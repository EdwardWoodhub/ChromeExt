**Article 1: "Taxing Time"**  
**Participants:** *Jason Furman (Former Obama Economic Adviser), Viktor Shvets (Macquarie Strategist), Author*  
---  
**Jason Furman:**  
"People don’t get it—these ‘reduced’ tariffs are still way higher than what any other major country’s imposing. It’s more inflationary now than on Liberation Day. This isn’t stabilizing things; it’s just the start of a mess."  

**Viktor Shvets:**  
(On Bloomberg’s podcast) "What’s the endgame here? Re-industrialize America? Fund tax cuts? Redirect global capital? Nah—they wanna remake America. But you can’t do that without remaking the *world*. It’s a revolution, man."  

**Author:**  
(Commenting) Trump’s tariffs were so slapdash, they taxed penguin-populated Antarctic islands. Markets tanked $6 trillion in days. Jamie Dimon warned of recession, and even GOP senators like Thom Tillis are asking, *"Whose throat do I choke if this blows up?"* But Trump’s just pivoting, not retreating—leaving 10% tariffs on most nations and slapping China with 100%. The White House thinks this’ll lure factories back, but businesses need stability, not 90-day policy whiplash.

**Article 2: "Been There Dept. – Market-Crash Survivors"**  
**Participants:** *Charlie Duncan (106-year-old survivor), Mariam Bailey (106-year-old survivor), Author/Interviewer*  
---  
**Charlie Duncan:**  
(Spitting snuff into a cup) "Back in ’29, Daddy woke us at 4:30 AM every damn day. Then the crash hit—cotton prices? Pennies. Couldn’t sell crops, couldn’t get hired. Dreamed of bein’ a country doc, but ended up drivin’ mules. Now? I keep my money in CDs. Ain’t riskin’ it."  
(On modern scams) "Got 45 calls one Friday! Told one lady, *‘If you want sex, you’re barkin’ up the wrong tree. That quit workin’ 25 years ago.’*"  

**Mariam Bailey:**  
(Sipping soup) "Depression hit our church hard. We just held on. My advice now? Same as then: *Life’s got ups and downs.* Cream of something today, huh?"  

**Author:**  
(Observing) Duncan’s survived wars, poverty, and Giuliani’s 2024 photo op. Bailey’s mantra? Stoic resilience. Both centenarians agree: markets crash, but you adapt.

**Article 3: "Dept. of Art Objects Unsparing"**  
**Participants:** *Robert Crumb (Cartoonist), Dan Nadel (Biographer/Curator), Author*  
---  
**Robert Crumb:**  
(Peering at his 1967 *Head #1* drawing) "Took me two days. Probably used a brush… or maybe a Rapidograph. Masochistic either way."  
(On Marsh’s art) "Those women—powerful legs, shelf-like bottoms. My kinda style."  
(On his biography) "Nadel’s a pest, but he found the guy who sold me bad LSD in ’66. Too bad the guy’s dead—wanted to confront him."  

**Dan Nadel:**  
(To Crumb) "Chose these artworks with *your* taste in mind. Muscly legs, grotesques… classic Crumb."  
(To author) "The book tackles his controversies head-on—racism, sexism. But Crumb’s own harshest critic."  

**Author:**  
Crumb mocks the art world’s obsession with his doodles: *"Bourgeois couples drool over my placemat sketches—food stains and all!"* For him, comics belong in print, not galleries: *"The thrill’s seeing it folded and stapled. That’s the real art."*

**Article 4: "Outer Borough – Brawn and Bravado"**  
**Participants:** *Michael Gandolfini (Actor), Author/Interviewer*  
---  
**Michael Gandolfini:**  
(On prepping for *Daredevil*) "Staten Island’s Mayor Fisk aide? Total Trumpian meathead. Spent a week here tailing a firefighter—drank at biker bars, rode the free ferry. Everyone’s miserable with that skyline view."  
(On *The Sopranos* prequel) "Watched the series for the first time with my ‘bonus dads.’ Tony’s bipolar—rage masking depression. Dad internalized it; I don’t do Method. Acting’s osmosis—play anger, and your brain rewires."  

**Author:**  
(Commenting) Gandolfini, at 25, channels his late father’s legacy without the baggage. For him, NYC’s grit beats L.A.’s faux-positivity: *"On the ferry, you’re depressed, hating your life—it’s perfect."*

**Article 5: "Good Ideas Dept. – Eye Level"**  
**Participants:** *David Byrne (Artist), Nate Kamp (Exhibition Manager), Author*  
---  
**David Byrne:**  
(Drawing skyscraper-headed families) "Stairwells are boring—let’s confront people with eyeballs at every landing!"  
(On Spiky Person) "Someone said it’s a Russian bear-hunting outfit. Nah—just a cuddly *Hellraiser*."  
(On pandemic art) "Drew dingbats to cope. Turns out, I was sketching body dysmorphia. Who knew?"  

**Nate Kamp:**  
(Projecting sketches) "Eyes next. Creepy, right?"  

**Byrne:**  
(Squeaking marker) "Radiating lines—like bike spokes! Or dark matter diagrams. Vera Rubin would approve."  

**Author:**  
Byrne’s murals blend whimsy and existential dread. For him, art’s a tool to *"think faster than math."* Even if it means lying on floors, drawing tears that look like the Brooklyn Bridge.
