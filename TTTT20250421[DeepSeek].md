**Article 1: "Taxing Time"**  
**Participants:** *Jason Furman (Former Obama Economic Adviser), Viktor Shvets (Macquarie Strategist), Author*  
---  
**Jason Furman:**  
"Look, folks aren’t grasping this—even the ‘reduced’ tariffs are still way higher than what any major economy’s slapping on imports. They’re *more* inflationary now than on Liberation Day! This isn’t calming things down—it’s kicking off a whole new era of chaos. Trump’s just swapping one dumpster fire for another: 10% tariffs on most countries, 100% on China. Markets are gonna keep yo-yoing till someone sane takes the wheel."  

**Viktor Shvets:**  
(Chuckling on Bloomberg’s podcast) "What’s the play here, huh? Rebuild U.S. factories? Fund tax cuts? Redirect cash flows? Nah—they’re after a full American *reboot*. But you can’t reboot America without rebooting the globe. It’s a revolution, whether the markets like it or not. Investors? They’re betting Trump’s bluffing. Treasury bonds tanking? That’s the real red flag."  

**Author:**  
(Interjecting) Let’s not forget the *genius* behind these tariffs. Antarctic penguins got taxed 10%! Cambodia—a dirt-poor textile hub—got hit with 50%. Markets nosedived $6 trillion in days. Jamie Dimon’s out here warning about recession, and GOP senators like Thom Tillis are literally asking, *“Whose throat do I choke if this blows up?”* But Trump’s calling markets “yippy” and doubling down. White House thinks high tariffs will magically lure factories back, but businesses need stability, not policy whiplash every 90 days.  

**Jason Furman:**  
(Adding) Exactly! You think CEOs are gonna build plants here when the rules change overnight? China and the EU are already buddying up. If this keeps up, the U.S. could lose its trade crown—and that 10% global trade flow? Poof.  

**Viktor Shvets:**  
(Leaning in) Worst part? Trump’s treating tariffs like a casino game. Threaten, pause, escalate—it’s all leverage for his ‘deal-making.’ But trade wars aren’t poker. Households could lose $4,700 a year. Sometimes *everyone* loses."  

**Author:**  
(Closing) Bottom line? This isn’t a reset—it’s a gamble with America’s economic dominance as the chip. Beijing’s already forming a “national team” to counterpunch. Axios put it best: *“The world’s hot new trade? ‘Sell America.’”*

**Article 2: "Been There Dept. – Market-Crash Survivors"**  
**Participants:** *Charlie Duncan (106-year-old survivor), Mariam Bailey (106-year-old survivor), Author/Interviewer*  
---  
**Charlie Duncan:**  
(Spitting snuff into a cup) "Back in ’29, Daddy woke us at 4:30 AM every damn day. Then the crash hit—cotton prices? Pennies. Couldn’t sell crops, couldn’t get hired. Dreamed of bein’ a country doc, but ended up drivin’ mules. Now? I keep my money in CDs. Ain’t riskin’ it."  
(On modern scams) "Got 45 calls one Friday! Told one lady, *‘If you want sex, you’re barkin’ up the wrong tree. That quit workin’ 25 years ago.’*"  

**Mariam Bailey:**  
(Sipping soup) "Depression hit our church hard. We just held on. My advice now? Same as then: *Life’s got ups and downs.* Cream of something today, huh?"  

**Author:**  
(Observing) Duncan’s survived wars, poverty, and Giuliani’s 2024 photo op. Bailey’s mantra? Stoic resilience. Both centenarians agree: markets crash, but you adapt.

**Article 3: "Dept. of Art Objects Unsparing"**  
**Participants:** *Robert Crumb (Cartoonist), Dan Nadel (Biographer/Curator), Author*  
---  
**Robert Crumb:**  
(Peering at his 1967 *Head #1* drawing) "Took me two days. Probably used a brush… or maybe a Rapidograph. Masochistic either way."  
(On Marsh’s art) "Those women—powerful legs, shelf-like bottoms. My kinda style."  
(On his biography) "Nadel’s a pest, but he found the guy who sold me bad LSD in ’66. Too bad the guy’s dead—wanted to confront him."  

**Dan Nadel:**  
(To Crumb) "Chose these artworks with *your* taste in mind. Muscly legs, grotesques… classic Crumb."  
(To author) "The book tackles his controversies head-on—racism, sexism. But Crumb’s own harshest critic."  

**Author:**  
Crumb mocks the art world’s obsession with his doodles: *"Bourgeois couples drool over my placemat sketches—food stains and all!"* For him, comics belong in print, not galleries: *"The thrill’s seeing it folded and stapled. That’s the real art."*
